# Willingness-to-Pay Validation Plan

## California PSPS-Affected Homeowners

**Objective:** Determine if customers in high-PSPS-frequency ZIP codes will pay $50-80/month for battery backup power priced as outage insurance.

**Success criterion:** ≥40% of respondents indicate willingness to pay ≥$60/mo for 48-hour outage coverage.

**Timeline:** 3-4 weeks (survey design + fielding + analysis)

**Budget:** $8k-15k (survey platform + incentives + analysis)

## Survey Instrument

### Screening Questions (30 seconds)

**Q1:** "Have you experienced a power outage in the last 12 months?"

- [ ] Yes, 1-2 times
- [ ] Yes, 3+ times (QUALIFY)
- [ ] No

**Q2:** "How long was your longest outage in the last 2 years?"

- [ ] Less than 1 hour
- [ ] 1-4 hours
- [ ] 4-12 hours
- [ ] 12-48 hours (QUALIFY)
- [ ] More than 2 days (STRONG QUALIFY)

**Q3:** "Have you heard of Public Safety Power Shutoffs (PSPS)?"

- [ ] Yes, very familiar
- [ ] Yes, somewhat familiar
- [ ] No

**ROUTE:** Only continue with respondents who experienced outages ≥4 hours OR are very familiar with PSPS.

### Problem Validation (2 minutes)

**Q4 (open-ended):** "Tell us about your worst power outage. What problems did it cause?"

_Scoring: Count mentions of_

- Food spoilage (refrigerator/freezer)
- Lost work/income
- Comfort (heat/AC, lighting)
- Medical needs (devices, medications)
- Safety concerns
- Other

**Q5:** "How much would you estimate you lost (in money, food, time) during your longest outage?"

- [ ] $0-50
- [ ] $50-200
- [ ] $200-500
- [ ] $500-1,000
- [ ] $1,000+

### Solution Introduction (1 minute)

**SHOW IMAGE:** Battery backup system in garage, silhouette of house with power symbol.

"A home battery system can provide backup power for 1-2 days during an outage. It stores power overnight when electricity is cheap, and provides 10+ hours of power during peak outage times (fridge, lights, WiFi, fans)."

**Q6:** "On a scale of 1-5, how interested are you in having backup power?"

- [ ] Not interested (1)
- [ ] Somewhat interested (2-3)
- [ ] Very interested (4-5)

**ROUTE:** Only continue with interest level 3+.

### Price Sensitivity (3 minutes)

**Q7 (Van Westendorp Price Ladder):** "At what monthly price would you consider a battery backup system..."

Present 5 price points on independent sliders (0-100):

- Too expensive (wouldn't buy at any price)
- Getting expensive (price is high)
- Good value (acceptable price)
- Bargain (great deal)
- Ideal price (best price)

Prices to test:

- $29/mo
- $49/mo
- $69/mo
- $89/mo
- $129/mo

**Scoring:** Identify optimal price (highest "good value" + "bargain" combined)

### Lease Frame (2 minutes)

**Q8:** "A battery system costs $13,500 installed. You could:"

- [ ] Buy it outright ($13,500 upfront, own it, no support)
- [ ] Lease it ($60/mo for 25 years, includes insurance, maintenance, support)

"Which appeals more?"

**Q9 (if lease selected):** "The lease includes 48-hour backup power during outages. At what monthly price would you definitely sign up?"

- [ ] $29/mo
- [ ] $39/mo
- [ ] $49/mo
- [ ] $59/mo
- [ ] $69/mo
- [ ] $79/mo
- [ ] $99/mo
- [ ] Wouldn't sign up

**Q10 (if lease rejected):** "What would change your mind?"

- [ ] Lower monthly price (to $___/mo)
- [ ] Longer coverage (48+ hours)
- [ ] Better warranty/guarantees
- [ ] Would rather own outright
- [ ] Nothing, not interested

### Demographic & Segmentation (2 minutes)

**Q11:** "What is your annual household income?"

- [ ] <$75k
- [ ] $75-150k
- [ ] $150-250k
- [ ] $250k+

**Q12:** "How long have you lived in your current home?"

- [ ] <1 year
- [ ] 1-3 years
- [ ] 3-10 years
- [ ] 10+ years

**Q13:** "Do you have solar panels on your home?"

- [ ] Yes
- [ ] No
- [ ] Planning to install

**Q14:** "What matters most in an outage?"

- [ ] Food preservation (refrigerator)
- [ ] Comfort (AC/heat)
- [ ] Communications (WiFi, phone)
- [ ] Work/income
- [ ] Medical equipment

## Survey Methodology

### Platform & Mechanics

- **Tool:** Qualtrics or SurveyMonkey (both support Van Westendorp pricing analysis)
- **Distribution:**
  - Online panels (Qualtrics, SurveyMonkey audiences)
  - Local Facebook ads targeted to ZIP codes
  - SDG&E customer lists (if accessible via partnership)
- **Incentive:** $5-10 Amazon gift card for completion
- **Completion time:** 8-10 minutes
- **Language options:** English + Spanish

### Sample Size

**Power analysis:**

- Null hypothesis: WTP ≥60/mo = 30% (insurance not viable)
- Alternative hypothesis: WTP ≥60/mo = 50% (insurance viable)
- Power: 80%, α: 0.05
- **Required n:** 320 per primary ZIP cohort

**Recommended strategy:**

- **Tier 1 deep dive:** 50 respondents per high-PSPS ZIP (6 ZIPs × 50 = 300)
- **Tier 2 screening:** 20 respondents per secondary ZIP (if expanded)
- **Total budget:** 300-400 responses

**Cost:** $3-5k survey fielding + $2-3k incentives = $5-8k

### Timeline

| Phase                  | Duration | Deliverable                               |
| ---------------------- | -------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Survey design & QA** | 1 week   | Qualtrics setup, price ladders tested     |
| **Fielding**           | 2 weeks  | 300-400 responses collected               |
| **Analysis**           | 1 week   | Graphs, Van Westendorp curves, WTP by ZIP |
| **Report**             | 3 days   | 10-page findings + recommendations        |

## Analysis & Success Criteria

### Primary KPI: WTP ≥60/mo

| Finding                      | Interpretation              | Next Step                                     |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **≥50% say yes to $60/mo**   | ✓ Insurance angle viable    | Pilot in top 3 ZIP codes                      |
| **30-50% say yes to $60/mo** | Marginal; $50/mo might work | Test $50/mo messaging in pilot                |
| **<30% say yes to $60/mo**   | Insurance angle fails       | Archive; focus on arbitrage or pivot entirely |

### Secondary KPIs

1. **Van Westendorp optimal price:** Where do "good value" + "bargain" intersect?
   - If <$50/mo → Need to reduce lease cost or raise perceived value
   - If $50-70/mo → Aligned with cost floor
   - If >$70/mo → Potential upside if volume supports it

2. **Lease vs buy preference:** What % choose lease frame?
   - <30% lease → Ownership preference strong; reframe as purchase option
   - 30-50% lease → Mixed; both paths viable
   - > 50% lease → Lease model has legs

3. **Problem resonance by segment:**
   - Which problem matters most? (Food, comfort, work, medical)
   - Does stated problem correlate with higher WTP?
   - **Use for messaging:** Lead with highest-resonance problem

4. **Price sensitivity by income:**
   - Does WTP increase with income?
   - Design two tiers? ($50/mo economy, $80/mo premium)

## Go/No-Go Decision Tree

### Go-Forward Conditions (Pilot Justified)

**If all three are true:**

1. ≥40% indicate WTP ≥$60/mo
2. Average Van Westendorp optimal price ≥$55/mo
3. ≥35% prefer lease frame

→ **Launch pilot:** 50-100 leads in top 3 ZIP codes, Zoom closes, $60-70/mo offer

### No-Go Conditions (Archive Insurance Angle)

**If any one is true:**

1. <30% indicate WTP ≥$60/mo
2. Van Westendorp optimal price <$45/mo
3. <20% prefer lease frame

→ **Archive insurance angle, pursue alternatives:**

- Arbitrage rebrand (already ruled out economically)
- Generator replacement messaging (different product class)
- Contractor channel (HVAC + battery bundles)

## Pilot Design (If Go-Forward)

### Micro-Pilot Scope

- **Duration:** 90 days
- **Target:** 50-100 qualified leads from top 3 ZIP codes (91942, 92026, 92084)
- **Offer:** $60-70/mo lease, 48-hour battery, outage insurance positioning
- **Channels:**
  - Facebook/Instagram ads targeted to ZIP + interest "power outage" OR "emergency preparedness"
  - Local SDG&E community partner (if available)
  - Nextdoor neighborhood ads

### Success Metrics for Pilot

| Metric               | Target               | Threshold                    |
| -------------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Lead cost (CPL)**  | <$50                 | Go/No-go at $100             |
| **Qualified rate**   | >40% of leads        | Adjust targeting if <25%     |
| **Appointment rate** | >30% of qualified    | Script/offer issue if <20%   |
| **Close rate**       | >20% of appointments | Sales enablement gap if <10% |
| **CAC**              | <$150/customer       | Model breaks at >$200        |

### Pilot Economics

- **50 leads @ $50 CPL:** $2,500 ad spend
- **20 qualified (40%):**
- **6 appointments (30%):**
- **1-2 closes (20%):**
- **CAC:** $1,250-2,500 per customer
- **Lease revenue/year:** $600-840 per customer
- **Payback period:** 1.5-4 years (depends on SG&A allocation)

## Risks & Mitigations

| Risk                                                         | Mitigation                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Sample bias:** Only engaged customers respond              | Use incentives; soft-launch bonus ($10 vs $5) to drive participation          |
| **PSPS recency:** Survey timing after major event skews high | Field survey over 2-3 weeks to average sentiment                              |
| **Lease resistance:** Cultural preference for ownership      | Frame lease as insurance, not financing; show maintenance burden of ownership |
| **Price anchor:** Lead price ladder point biases responses   | Randomize price ladder order; use both ascending and descending versions      |
